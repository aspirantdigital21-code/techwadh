The **Staff Selection Commission Combined Graduate Level (SSC CGL) Examination** is one of India's major recruitment examinations for graduates. It is conducted to fill Group B and Group C posts in central government ministries, departments and organisations.

If you are planning to prepare for SSC CGL, this guide explains the posts, eligibility, age limits, exam pattern, syllabus, pay levels and a practical preparation strategy. It also covers the current status of the 2026 examination.

> **Information updated: 21 November 2026.** Dates and rules below are based on the SSC CGL 2026 official notification and subsequent SSC notices. Always check the latest notice on the official SSC website before acting.

## What is SSC CGL?

SSC CGL is a national-level competitive examination conducted by the Staff Selection Commission. Recruitment is made for different roles, including:

- Assistant Section Officer
- Inspector of Income Tax
- Inspector in Central Excise and other CBIC formations
- Assistant Enforcement Officer
- Sub-Inspector in CBI and other departments
- Auditor and Accountant
- Tax Assistant
- Junior Statistical Officer (JSO)
- Statistical Investigator Grade-II

The exact posts and vacancies can vary from one recruitment cycle to another. Some posts also have specific physical, medical, computer-proficiency or educational requirements.

## SSC CGL eligibility criteria

### 1. Educational qualification

For most SSC CGL posts, candidates need a **bachelor's degree from a recognised university or equivalent**.

Some posts have additional subject requirements:

- **Junior Statistical Officer (JSO):** A bachelor's degree in any subject with at least 60% marks in Mathematics at Class 12 level, or a bachelor's degree with Statistics as a subject at degree level.
- **Statistical Investigator Grade-II:** A bachelor's degree with the specified subject background, such as Statistics, Mathematics, Economics, Demography, Population Studies, Operations Research, IT, Computer Science, Computer Applications, Data Science or Artificial Intelligence, as listed in the notification.
- **Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer:** A bachelor's degree is the essential qualification in the 2026 notice. Professional or postgraduate qualifications such as CA, CMA, CS, M.Com, MBA (Finance) and related qualifications are listed as desirable, not as the basic degree requirement.

Candidates who are in the final year can apply only if they obtain the required qualification by the cut-off date specified in the notification. For SSC CGL 2026, the educational qualification cut-off date was **1 August 2026**.

### 2. Age limit

The age limit depends on the post. For SSC CGL 2026, the notification specified these common age brackets, calculated as on **1 August 2026**:

- 18–27 years
- 18–30 years
- 20–30 years
- 18–32 years

Age relaxation is available to eligible categories under government rules. For example, the standard upper-age relaxation is 5 years for SC/ST and 3 years for OBC, subject to the conditions in the notification. PwBD and other eligible categories have separate provisions.

Do not assume that one age limit applies to every SSC CGL post. Check the post-wise table and the applicable category rules in the official notice.

## SSC CGL exam pattern

The SSC CGL 2026 selection process has two computer-based examination tiers: **Tier-I and Tier-II**.

### Tier-I exam pattern

Tier-I is an objective multiple-choice examination with four sections.

| Subject | Questions | Marks |
|---|---:|---:|
| General Intelligence and Reasoning | 25 | 50 |
| General Awareness | 25 | 50 |
| Quantitative Aptitude | 25 | 50 |
| English Comprehension | 25 | 50 |
| **Total** | **100** | **200** |

- Total time: 60 minutes.
- Sectional timer: 15 minutes for each subject.
- Negative marking: 0.50 marks for each wrong answer.
- Questions are set in English and Hindi, except the English Comprehension section.

Tier-I is used to shortlist candidates for Tier-II. The Commission uses normalized scores where examinations are held in multiple shifts.

### Tier-II exam pattern

Tier-II is important because final merit for most posts is based on performance in Tier-II, subject to the applicable qualifying requirements and post preferences.

**Paper-I is compulsory for all posts.** It includes:

- Mathematical Abilities
- Reasoning and General Intelligence
- English Language and Comprehension
- General Awareness
- Computer Knowledge Test
- Data Entry Speed Test (DEST)

Paper-I is conducted in two sessions on the same day. The first session includes Mathematics, Reasoning, English, General Awareness and the Computer Knowledge Test. The second session is the Data Entry Speed Test.

Additional papers apply to specific posts:

- **Paper-II (Statistics):** For candidates shortlisted for Junior Statistical Officer and Statistical Investigator Grade-II posts.
- **Paper-III (General Studies – Finance and Economics):** For candidates shortlisted for Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer posts.

There is negative marking in the relevant objective sections of Tier-II. The 2026 notification specifies 1 mark for each wrong answer in Sections I, II and III of Paper-I, and 0.50 marks for each wrong answer in Paper-II and Paper-III. Check the detailed scheme in the notification for the exact section-wise rules.

## SSC CGL syllabus: What should you study?

### 1. Quantitative Aptitude / Mathematical Abilities

Important areas include:

- Number system and arithmetic
- Percentage, ratio and proportion
- Average, profit and loss
- Simple and compound interest
- Time and work, time and distance
- Algebra and geometry
- Mensuration and trigonometry
- Data interpretation

### 2. General Intelligence and Reasoning

Prepare topics such as:

- Analogy and classification
- Series and coding-decoding
- Venn diagrams
- Blood relations and direction sense
- Syllogism and logical reasoning
- Non-verbal reasoning
- Problem-solving and analytical questions

### 3. English Language and Comprehension

Focus on:

- Grammar and sentence structure
- Vocabulary and word usage
- Synonyms and antonyms
- Idioms and phrases
- Error spotting and sentence improvement
- Cloze test and fill in the blanks
- Reading comprehension

### 4. General Awareness

Cover a balanced mix of:

- History, Geography and Polity
- Indian Economy
- General Science
- Static General Knowledge
- Important national and international events
- Government schemes and key developments

For Tier-II, also prepare Computer Knowledge and DEST. Candidates targeting JSO, Statistical Investigator or AAO/AAO posts should study the additional paper relevant to their chosen post.

## How to prepare for SSC CGL

A consistent plan is more useful than collecting too many books or study resources. Use this approach:

1. **Understand the syllabus:** Read the official syllabus and identify topics you already know and topics that need work.
2. **Build the basics:** Study Mathematics, English and Reasoning topic by topic before moving to advanced questions.
3. **Practise General Awareness daily:** Revise static GK and follow reliable current-affairs sources.
4. **Solve previous-year papers:** Learn the question style, difficulty level and time pressure.
5. **Take timed mock tests:** Analyse every mock test. Record accuracy, time spent and weak topics.
6. **Practise computer skills:** Do not ignore the Computer Knowledge Test and DEST required in Tier-II.
7. **Revise regularly:** Keep short notes for formulas, grammar rules, facts and mistakes.

### A simple weekly routine

| Activity | Suggested frequency |
|---|---|
| Mathematics | Daily |
| Reasoning | Daily |
| English | Daily |
| General Awareness | Daily revision |
| Previous-year questions | 4–5 days a week |
| Mock test and analysis | 1–2 tests a week initially; increase as needed |
| Computer and typing practice | 2–3 sessions a week |

Adjust the hours according to your current level and available study time. Accuracy and careful analysis matter more than simply attempting a large number of questions.

## How to apply for SSC CGL

Applications are submitted online through the official SSC website. Candidates need to complete One-Time Registration (OTR) on the current SSC portal before applying for an examination.

For a future cycle:

1. Open [SSC's official website](https://ssc.gov.in/).
2. Complete or check your One-Time Registration.
3. Read the latest CGL notification carefully.
4. Check post-wise age, education and physical/medical requirements.
5. Fill in the application and verify every detail before submission.
6. Pay the fee if applicable and save the application confirmation.

The SSC CGL 2026 notification listed an application fee of ₹100. Women, SC, ST, eligible PwBD and eligible Ex-Servicemen candidates were exempt under the notification's conditions. Fee rules may change in a future cycle.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SSC CGL conducted every year?

SSC generally includes CGL in its annual examination calendar, but candidates should confirm each cycle's notification and schedule on the official SSC website.

### Can a final-year graduation student apply?

The 2026 notice allowed candidates who had appeared in their final-year examination, provided they acquired the required qualification by 1 August 2026. The cut-off date can differ in future notifications.

### Is there negative marking in SSC CGL?

Yes. In Tier-I, 0.50 marks are deducted for each wrong answer. Tier-II has its own negative-marking rules, which vary by paper and section.

### Is SSC CGL only for candidates with a Mathematics degree?

No. Most posts require a bachelor's degree in any discipline. Specific statistical posts have additional subject or marks requirements.

### Does every SSC CGL post require a physical test?

No. Physical or medical standards apply only to certain posts. Check the post-wise requirements before selecting preferences.

SSC CGL offers graduates an opportunity to compete for a range of central government jobs. The right preparation starts with understanding the official syllabus, checking post-specific eligibility and practising consistently.

For the 2026 cycle, Tier-I has been conducted and the answer-key challenge window has closed. Candidates should keep checking SSC for the Tier-I result and any update to the tentative December Tier-II schedule.
