## What should you check before buying a budget phone?

A long specification sheet can be misleading. These are the checks that matter more in daily use.

### 1. Choose at least 6GB RAM if your budget allows

For a phone you plan to keep for a few years, 6GB RAM and 128GB storage are a sensible starting point. A 4GB model can still work for calls, messaging and light use, but heavy multitasking may feel restrictive sooner.

### 2. Do not judge a camera by megapixels alone

A 50MP label does not tell you how well a phone handles skin tones, indoor lighting, motion or video. Look for real sample photos and reviews. A useful main camera is usually more valuable than several low-resolution extra sensors.

### 3. Check the display type and resolution

An AMOLED panel generally offers deeper blacks and punchier contrast. A Full HD+ LCD can still be sharp and perfectly usable. If you read a lot or watch video, compare display resolution and outdoor brightness—not just the refresh-rate number.

### 4. Battery capacity is not the whole story

A 7,000mAh battery can be useful, but actual endurance also depends on the processor, display, signal strength and software. Check charging wattage and whether a compatible charger is included.

### 5. Read the software-update policy

Look for the number of Android version upgrades and the security-update period. A phone that receives updates for longer can remain safer and more useful for longer.

### 6. Check 5G bands, warranty and service access

Make sure the phone supports the 5G bands used by your operator and that the seller is authorised. Check the warranty, return window and nearby service options before paying.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is the best budget smartphone in India?

There is no single best choice for everyone. The Realme P4x 5G and Moto G67 Power 5G are worth comparing for battery and performance, while the Samsung Galaxy M17 5G is more relevant if software support is your priority.

### Is 5G necessary in a budget smartphone?

If you plan to keep the phone for three or more years and have 5G coverage, buying a compatible 5G phone is sensible. If your usage is basic and your area has limited 5G coverage, prioritise overall quality, storage and battery instead.

### How much RAM and storage should I buy?

For most people, 6GB RAM and 128GB storage are a good starting point. Choose more storage if you record lots of videos, install large games or keep files offline.

### Should I buy a phone during a sale?

A sale can make a good phone better value, but compare the final price across authorised sellers. Check the exact variant, bank conditions, exchange requirements and return policy.
