Sri Lanka A chased **318 in 41.3 overs** to beat India A by **eight wickets** in the first semi-final of the 2026 ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars in Doha. India A made **317/8** after choosing to bat. Lasith Croospulle hit **116 off 107**, and Player of the Match Sineth Jayawardena struck **105 off 70**. Their **174-run opening stand** put Sri Lanka A on course for the final with **51 balls to spare**.

## How India A went from 159/1 to 235/6

Vaibhav Sooryavanshi and Abhishek Sharma put on **80 in eight overs**. Sooryavanshi hit seven boundaries in his 27-ball 40, and India A still had 95 runs on the board after the first ten overs. Sharma and captain Tilak Varma then added 79 for the second wicket.

Sharma's dismissal at 159 in the 22nd over gave Sri Lanka A a route back into the innings. From **159/1 immediately before that wicket**, India A lost five wickets for 76 runs. Ravindu Fernando trapped both Varma and Rinku Singh lbw, while Nipun Dhananjaya took both of his wickets while conceding 41 in ten overs.

At 235/6, Shahbaz Ahmed and Tanush Kotian added **73 off 55 balls**. Shahbaz hit four sixes in an unbeaten 54 and helped India A reach 317. Janith Liyanage broke the opening stand and later removed Kotian.

## How Sri Lanka A chased 318 with 51 balls left

Sri Lanka A took **85 runs from the first ten overs** without losing a wicket. The required rate started at **6.36 per over** and fell to **5.05** after the powerplay. Jayawardena hit ten fours and six sixes, scoring at a strike rate of **150**, before Shahbaz dismissed him at 174/1 after 21.3 overs.

Croospulle and Nishan Madushka then added **91 from 83 balls**. When Harshit Rana removed Croospulle at 265/2 after 35.2 overs, Sri Lanka A needed only **53 from 88 balls**. Madushka and captain Pavan Rathnayake finished the chase with an unbeaten **53-run stand off 37 balls**. Rathnayake hit three sixes in his 16-ball 31.

## The decisive difference

India A scored **10 more runs** in the first powerplay, but Sri Lanka A carried its opening stand **94 runs further**. That change in wicket preservation meant Sri Lanka A could keep attacking without the middle-order rebuild India A needed. Jayawardena set the pace, Croospulle batted past the 35th over, and Madushka and Rathnayake closed out the chase.
